Facade Surface Studio 曲面幕牆工作室

Bend a podium skin by hand in 3D, and watch it panelise as you go. Drag the control points and the surface bulges; drag an attractor and the openings swell around it; let curvature, the sun, a viewer or height share the say. Then rationalise thousands of panels into a handful of types, check planarity and curvature on heat maps, and export the model and a flattened sheet of the panel types. The kind of definition that used to need Rhino and Grasshopper, in your browser.

How it works
  1. Surface. The footprint is a closed curve measured along its length. A lattice of control values (8 around by 4 up to start) pushes the skin out or in and lifts it, blended by a cubic B-spline (or straight lines for a faceted, folded skin), with an optional twist and taper towards the top.
  2. Divide. The surface is cut into U columns around and V rows up, measured on the surface parameters, so panels follow the bulges.
  3. Pattern. Each cell becomes a quad, two triangles, a diamond, a hexagon, a perforated panel with one round hole, a vertical fin, or a four-sided pyramid.
  4. Driver. Every panel gets a value from 0 to 1 from each driver: closeness to the attractor points and the curve, curvature, sun exposure (face normal against the sun direction on the 21st of the month, solar time), distance to or facing the viewer, and height. The values are averaged with your weights and set the opening size, fin angle or pyramid height.
  5. Rationalise. k-means clustering groups the panels into N types by edge lengths, diagonal and opening size. With snapping on, each panel takes its type's opening. The tool reports the largest deviation from the type, in millimetres, and the share of panels within your tolerance.
  6. Analyse. Planarity: for four-sided panels, the distance of the fourth corner from the plane of the other three; for hexagons, the largest distance from the best-fit plane. Gaussian and mean curvature come from the surface's derivatives.
  7. Export. Any change re-runs only the steps after it, like a Grasshopper definition. The exports are made from what you see.
Limits
  • Concept stageThe panel types are a concept-stage rationalisation, not fabrication data. Joints, thickness, fixings, brackets, tolerances and structure are for your facade engineer and fabricator.
  • PlanarityDoubly curved quads are not flat. The tool shows how far out they are; it does not make them planar. A planarisation solver is not part of this version.
  • SunThe sun driver compares each panel's direction with one sun position. It is not a solar radiation or daylight result and ignores neighbouring buildings and self-shading.
  • RegulationsNothing here checks fire, wind load, ventilation, glare or any other statutory requirement.
  • PhonesOn phones the panel count is capped and the model updates when you lift your finger.
