Good grief 合宜的哀慟
(Lamentations 耶利米哀歌 2:1-22)

Lamentations 2:1-22 — 9th August 2026耶利米哀歌 2:1-22 — 2026 年 8 月 9 日
Good grief.合宜的哀慟
Big idea: Everything of value has been lost, due to the angry judgement of God, and all that anyone remaining can do is cry out when confronted by the enormity of what has happened.中心信息：一切寶貴的都已失喪，這是出於神忿怒的審判；面對眼前這場難以想像的浩劫，倖存的人唯一能做的，就是放聲向神哀哭呼求。
………………………………
"Jerusalem the city-woman may have sunk into sobbing silence (1:22c), but the poet has plenty left." (Wright, p.77)「耶路撒冷這位城中的婦人，或許已經哭到沉默無聲（1 章 22 節下），但詩人心中仍有許多話未說完。」（賴特 Wright，第 77 頁）
Last week we began to learn the lessons of what happened in 586 BC — to the city on a hill, Jerusalem, when she was cut down by the mighty Babylonians.上週我們開始學習主前 586 年所發生之事的功課——那座山上之城耶路撒冷，如何被強盛的巴比倫人所砍倒。
There is so much more to say, as the poet sits in the smoking rubble and ruin of a once great city.然而，當詩人坐在這座昔日偉大城市那冒着煙的頹垣敗瓦之中，他仍有許多話要說。
Because the destruction is total and the catastrophe is unimaginable. So the lamenting continues — even though he could see it coming, even though he pleaded with the people for it not to happen.因為毀滅是徹底的，災難是無法想像的。所以哀歌仍要繼續——縱然他早已預見這一切，縱然他曾苦苦哀求百姓，求他們不要走到這一步。
As we sit with him and look around us, we too need this good grief — and we need to see Jesus.當我們與他一同坐下，環顧四周，我們同樣需要這種合宜的哀慟——我們也需要看見耶穌。
This is the second poem. Three big things.這是第二首哀歌。當中有三件大事。
………………………………
1. The anger of the Lord一、耶和華的怒氣
"It is a dreadful thing to fall into the hands of the living God." (Hebrews 10:31)「落在永生神的手裏，真是可怕的！」（希伯來書 10:31）
Chapter 2 is, above everything else, a graphic illustration of exactly that. The day of God's pitiless anger had finally arrived.第二章最主要的，就是把這句話活生生地描繪出來。神那毫不顧惜的忿怒之日，終於臨到了。
Read v.1-2, v.21 and v.22.請讀 1 至 2 節、21 節、22 節。
"How the Lord has covered Daughter Zion with the cloud of his anger! He has hurled down the splendour of Israel from heaven to earth; he has not remembered his footstool in the day of his anger. Without pity the Lord has swallowed up all the dwellings of Jacob…" (v.1-2)「主何竟發怒，使黑雲遮蔽錫安城！他將以色列的華美從天扔在地上；在他發怒的日子並不記念自己的腳凳。主吞滅雅各一切的住處，並不顧惜⋯⋯」（1 至 2 節）
"…you have slain them in the day of your anger; you have slaughtered them without pity. … In the day of the Lord's anger no one escaped or survived." (v.21-22)「⋯⋯你發怒的日子殺死他們。你殺了，並不顧惜。⋯⋯耶和華發怒的日子，無人逃脫，無人存留。」（21 至 22 節）
This is what it looks like and feels like when the anger of God at sin can no longer be held back. The dam bursts.當神對罪的怒氣再也按捺不住的時候，看起來、感覺起來就是這個樣子。水壩決堤了。
A frequent image in the Old Testament is God as warrior — fighting for his people. Classically, at the Exodus.舊約中一個常見的圖像，是神作勇士——為祂的子民爭戰。最典型的，就是出埃及的時候。
"The Lord will fight for you; you need only to be still." (Exodus 14:14)「耶和華必為你們爭戰；你們只管靜默，不要作聲。」（出埃及記 14:14）
If they had continued in repentance and faith, it would always have been so. But should they turn away — defy him and deny him, commit spiritual adultery, ignore his pleas through the prophets to return — then he would have to turn his fire on them.只要他們持守悔改與信心，這話就永遠有效。但他們若轉離祂——公然抗拒祂、否認祂，犯屬靈的姦淫，對先知一再的呼籲置若罔聞——那麼祂就必須把烈火轉向他們自己。
And this is what that looks like.而這，就是那個景象。
Note that in v.1-8 the Lord is the subject of every verb. He is the one doing this. The human enemy — the Babylonians — is not even mentioned until v.16.請留意，在 1 至 8 節中，耶和華是每一個動詞的主語。做這事的是祂自己。至於人的仇敵——巴比倫人——直到 16 節才被提及。
Isaiah 28:21 calls it his "strange work".以賽亞書 28:21 稱這是祂「非常的工」、「奇異的事」。
In many ways that is exactly right. Note that ten times he calls her his daughter — Daughter Judah, Daughter Zion. Their temple was his temple.從許多方面看，這正是實情。請留意，祂十次稱她為自己的女兒——猶大的女子、錫安的女子。他們的聖殿，就是祂的聖殿。
Read v.6 and v.7.請讀 6 節和 7 節。
"He has laid waste his dwelling like a garden; he has destroyed his place of meeting. The Lord has made Zion forget her appointed festivals and her Sabbaths… The Lord has rejected his altar and abandoned his sanctuary." (v.6-7)「他強取自己的帳幕，好像是園中的窩棚，毀壞他的聚會之處。耶和華使聖節和安息日在錫安都被忘記⋯⋯耶和華丟棄自己的祭壇，憎惡自己的聖所。」（6 至 7 節）
God levelled his own temple. He scattered his own people. He ruined his own city.神親手夷平自己的聖殿。祂親手驅散自己的子民。祂親手毀壞自己的城。
No mercy (v.1-5). No worship (v.6-7). No walls (v.8-9a).沒有憐憫（1 至 5 節）。沒有敬拜（6 至 7 節）。沒有城牆（8 至 9 節上）。
Heart-breaking and gut-wrenching — but richly deserved. Idolatry. Immorality. Injustice. Corruption. The city and the nation were ripe for judgement.這實在令人心碎、肝腸寸斷——但卻是罪有應得。拜偶像、道德敗壞、不公不義、貪贓枉法。這城、這國，已經熟透了，只等審判。
The "great and dreadful day of the Lord" (Malachi 4:5) — a preview of the greater one still to come upon the whole world (2 Peter 3:10; 1 Thessalonians 5:2). All of us need to be prepared for it.這是「耶和華大而可畏之日」（瑪拉基書 4:5）——也是將來臨到全世界那更大之日的預演（彼得後書 3:10；帖撒羅尼迦前書 5:2）。我們每一個人都必須為那日作好準備。
What do we make of God being angry? Is it unworthy of God?我們該怎樣看待「神發怒」這回事？這是否有損神的尊榮？
Robin Ham, in his devotional, tells the story of the Croatian theologian Miroslav Volf. Volf used to think that the "anger of God" was unworthy of him. But then, in the 1990s, his own people faced the terror and cruelty of a brutal and bloody war in the former Yugoslavia. Suddenly he found his views shifting — in a surprising way.韓恩（Robin Ham）在他的靈修書中，講述克羅地亞神學家沃弗（Miroslav Volf）的故事。沃弗從前認為「神的憤怒」有損神的尊榮。可是到了九十年代，他自己的同胞在前南斯拉夫經歷了一場殘暴血腥戰爭的恐怖與凶殘。突然之間，他發覺自己的看法起了變化——而且是出人意表的變化。
"I came to think that I would have to rebel against a God who wasn't wrathful at the sight of the world's evil. God isn't wrathful in spite of being love. God is wrathful because God is love." (Ham, p.53)「我開始認為，若有一位神看見世上的罪惡卻不發怒，我必要起來反抗這樣的神。神並不是『雖然是愛，卻仍發怒』；神發怒，正因為神就是愛。」（韓恩，第 53 頁）
God is not "nice". The Bible says he is good, and that he is love. That is why he must be angry at sin and must not let it win.神並不是「好好先生」。聖經說祂是「良善」的，說祂就是「愛」。正因如此，祂必須向罪發怒，絕不容讓罪得勝。
And that is why the only answer to all of this is the cross of Christ. Wrath and mercy meet. The Judge was judged. So that there can now be…也正因如此，這一切的唯一答案，就是基督的十字架。在那裏，忿怒與憐憫相遇。審判者自己被審判。以致如今可以說——
"Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus." (Romans 8:1)「如今，那些在基督耶穌裏的就不定罪了。」（羅馬書 8:1）
Question: where are you today? Question: the only place you can find refuge from him is in him — do you see it?問題：你今天在哪裏？問題：唯一能躲避祂忿怒的地方，就是躲進祂裏面——你看見了嗎？
………………………………
2. The grief of the prophet二、先知的悲痛
The prophet knows this is right and just — yet the grief and the pain are still overwhelming.先知知道這是公義的、是應當的——但那份悲傷與痛楚，仍然叫他承受不住。
Read v.11-13.請讀 11 至 13 節。
"My eyes fail from weeping, I am in torment within; my heart is poured out on the ground because my people are destroyed, because children and infants faint in the streets of the city." (v.11)「我眼中流淚，以致失明；我的心腸擾亂，肝膽塗地，都因我眾民遭毀滅，又因孩童和吃奶的在城內街上發昏。」（11 節）
"Your wound is as deep as the sea. Who can heal you?" (v.13)「因為你的裂口大如海，誰能醫治你呢？」（13 節）
The prophet weeps and convulses and mourns and laments over a scene that is almost too much to bear.先知痛哭、抽搐、悲哀、哀號，因為眼前的景象幾乎叫人無法承受。
Note that none of his tears are for himself — even though he is caught up in it all. This city is his city. These people are his people. And it is too much.請留意，他的眼淚沒有一滴是為自己流的——縱然他自己也身陷其中。這城是他的城。這群人是他的同胞。這一切，實在太沉重了。
Jeremiah was the "weeping prophet" before this even happened. But now, more than ever.早在這事發生之前，耶利米就已經是那位「流淚的先知」。但如今，更甚於從前。
If his anger is directed anywhere, it is at the false teachers and prophets. They failed.若說他的怒氣有所指向，那就是指向假教師和假先知。他們失職了。
Read v.14.請讀 14 節。
"The visions of your prophets were false and worthless; they did not expose your sin to ward off your captivity." (v.14)「你的先知為你見虛假和愚昧的異象，並沒有顯露你的罪孽，使你被擄的歸回。」（14 節）
The job of the prophets was to expose evil and unbelief and to bring people back to God. But they just played along. Why?先知的職分，是揭露罪惡與不信，把人帶回神面前。但他們只懂隨波逐流。為甚麼？
One — scared of being unpopular? Two — an easy life? Three — they just didn't care? Four, and most likely — they didn't believe it themselves.一、怕不受歡迎？二、貪圖安逸？三、根本漠不關心？四，也是最有可能的——他們自己根本就不相信。
Whatever the reason, they were a big part of why this happened — to their eternal shame. Those who teach "will be judged more strictly" (James 3:1).無論原因是甚麼，他們都是這場災難的一大成因——這是他們永遠的羞恥。作教導的人「要受更重的判斷」（雅各書 3:1）。
Question: do you weep, mourn and grieve when you see the state the world is in?問題：當你看見世界今日的光景，你有沒有哭泣、哀慟、憂傷？
"The wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against all the godlessness and wickedness of people." (Romans 1:18)「神的忿怒從天上顯明在一切不虔不義的人身上。」（羅馬書 1:18）
Mainly this is God "handing us over" — so that we reap what we sow.這主要是神「任憑」我們——叫我們自食其果，種甚麼就收甚麼。
We watch the news, so we know how to lament. Do you? For example…我們天天看新聞，所以我們是懂得哀慟的。你呢？舉例來說⋯⋯
Question: do you mourn and grieve over the wreckage of family breakdown? Think of the misery it causes — mental health in teenagers, the burden on the welfare state, increasing poverty, the abuse of children, alcoholism, loneliness.問題：你有沒有為家庭破碎所留下的殘局而哀傷？想想它帶來的種種苦楚——青少年的精神健康、社會福利制度的重擔、貧窮加劇、兒童受虐、酗酒、孤單。
Almost always, sin is at the root. But the question is: are our hearts still heavy with grief?幾乎每一件事的根源，都是罪。但問題是：我們的心，是否仍然為此沉重憂傷？
Question: what about the decline in the influence of Christianity, and the number of churches that have closed?問題：基督教影響力的衰退、一間又一間教會的關門，又如何呢？
On holiday in Cornwall, in Bodmin, we were looking for lunch. I googled it. It looked nice, and not too expensive. It turned out to be a former Wesleyan chapel — 1840? A big balcony. Seating for 200? As I sat there I couldn't help imagining the place thronged with worshippers. I didn't enjoy the meal.有一次在康和郡（Cornwall）的博德明（Bodmin）度假，我們想找地方吃午飯。我上網搜尋，看來不錯，價錢也不算貴。原來那是一座昔日的衛斯理宗禮拜堂——大概建於 1840 年吧？有一個很大的樓座，大概可坐 200 人？我坐在那裏，忍不住想像當年這裏擠滿敬拜者的情景。那一頓飯，我吃得毫無滋味。
Question: are you ever shocked, ever grieved, at the number of churches now turned into luxury apartments, nightclubs, trendy wine bars? Because the Bible was abandoned and the gospel was lost, it is no surprise. But do we lament it?問題：看見那麼多教會如今變成豪華公寓、夜總會、時尚酒吧，你可曾感到震驚、感到悲痛？由於他們離棄了聖經、失去了福音，這樣的結局並不出奇。但我們有沒有為此哀慟？
Question: do you know that our Saviour, Jesus, was a "weeping prophet" too?問題：你知道我們的救主耶穌，也是一位「流淚的先知」嗎？
As he approached the rebuilt city three hundred years later, he could see history repeating itself — a terrible judgement coming in AD 70 at the hands of the Romans. And he wept (Luke 19:41-44).三百年後，當祂走近那座重建的城，祂看見歷史正在重演——主後 70 年，一場可怕的審判將要藉羅馬人的手臨到。於是祂哭了（路加福音 19:41-44）。
"As he approached Jerusalem and saw the city, he wept over it and said, 'If you, even you, had only known on this day what would bring you peace — but now it is hidden from your eyes.'" (Luke 19:41-42)「耶穌快到耶路撒冷，看見城，就為它哀哭，說：巴不得你在這日子知道關係你平安的事；無奈這事現在是隱藏的，叫你的眼看不出來。」（路加福音 19:41-42）
Question: would we have wept?問題：換了是我們，我們會哭嗎？
But his tender heart is still alive to us today. If we are his, he feels for us — even when it is our own sin and stupidity that has brought us down.但祂那顆溫柔的心，今日對我們依然火熱。我們若是屬祂的，祂就體恤我們——即使把我們擊倒的，正是我們自己的罪和愚昧。
"Your very sins move him more to pity than to anger." (Thomas Goodwin)「你的罪不但不會惹動祂的怒氣，反倒更激起祂的憐憫。」（古德溫 Thomas Goodwin）
When you have messed up, don't stay away from him. Bring it to him. He is a sympathetic high priest (Hebrews 4).當你跌倒犯錯，不要躲開祂。把它帶到祂面前。祂是一位體恤我們的大祭司（希伯來書 4 章）。

3. The cries of the people三、百姓的哀求
He ends his own lament by urging and exhorting those who are left to cry out to God.先知在自己的哀歌末了，極力勸勉那些餘剩的人向神呼求。
Read v.18-19.請讀 18 至 19 節。
"Arise, cry out in the night, as the watches of the night begin; pour out your heart like water in the presence of the Lord. Lift up your hands to him for the lives of your children." (v.19)「夜間，每逢交更的時候要起來呼喊，在主面前傾心如水。⋯⋯你要為他們的性命向主舉手禱告。」（19 節）
Not to just sit there. Not to bottle it up. Not to give in to despair. Not to go quietly into this dark night. But to voice their pain and anguish and give it to God.不是呆坐着不動。不是把痛苦埋在心裏。不是向絕望屈服。不是默默地走進這漫漫長夜。而是要把痛苦與哀傷說出來，交給神。
So with what little energy and strength she has left, she does. It is not an easy listen.於是，她用僅餘的一點力氣，這樣做了。那並不是容易入耳的聲音。
Read v.20-21.請讀 20 至 21 節。
What are we expecting, or hoping for, given the context and the backstory? To put sin on the table? To beg for forgiveness? To plead for rescue and deliverance? No.按着上文下理和前因後果，我們期望聽見甚麼？把罪攤出來認？苦苦哀求赦免？懇切呼求拯救？都不是。
"It is more protest than petition, more reproach than repentance." (Jonathan Gibson, p.848)「這與其說是祈求，不如說是抗議；與其說是悔改，不如說是責問。」（吉遜 Jonathan Gibson，第 848 頁）
And yet — here it is, in our Bibles, for us.然而——它就這樣被記在聖經裏，留給我們。
Question: at the heart of biblical lament is "godly complaint". What do you make of that? Uncomfortable? It is biblical.問題：聖經式哀慟的核心，是一種「敬虔的申訴」。你怎樣看？覺得不舒服嗎？但這是合乎聖經的。
The psalmists ask: How long, O Lord? Why, O Lord? Surely not, Lord?詩人這樣問：「耶和華啊，要到幾時呢？」「耶和華啊，為甚麼呢？」「主啊，不會吧？」
Question: do we sometimes have to ask those questions? You can see why they fit here. If we are not asking them, we are not really seeing.問題：我們是否有時候必須問這些問題？你能明白為何這些問題放在這裏是合宜的。我們若不曾這樣問，就是我們根本沒有真正看見。
First — how extreme it all is. A vision of hell.第一，這一切何等極端。簡直是地獄的景象。
Read v.20.請讀 20 節。
"Look, Lord, and consider: whom have you ever treated like this? Should women eat their offspring, the children they have cared for? Should priest and prophet be killed in the sanctuary of the Lord?" (v.20)「耶和華啊，求你觀看！見你向誰這樣行？婦人豈可吃自己所生育手裏所搖弄的嬰孩嗎？祭司和先知豈可在主的聖所中被殺戮嗎？」（20 節）
It is like a horror film. Question: did it need to be this bad? What had the children done? It feels wrong.這簡直像一齣恐怖片。問題：非要慘烈到這個地步不可嗎？那些孩子做錯了甚麼？這感覺是不對的。
Second — the question of justice.第二，公義的問題。
The city has been decimated, ravaged and slaughtered by an enemy who by any measure is bloodthirsty and wicked. The Babylonians were a brutal military force. If anything, they were worse. And here they are given free rein to do whatever they want.這城被一個無論以任何標準衡量都嗜血邪惡的仇敵所摧毀、蹂躪、屠殺。巴比倫人是一支殘暴的軍隊。若真要比較，他們甚至更壞。而在這裏，他們卻被容許為所欲為。
At the end of chapter 1, Lady Zion cries out — what about them?在第一章的末了，錫安這位婦人放聲呼求——那他們呢？
"Let all their wickedness come before you; deal with them as you have dealt with me because of all my sins. My groans are many and my heart is faint." (1:22)「願他們的惡行都呈在你面前；你怎樣因我的一切罪過待我，求你照樣待他們；因我歎息甚多，心中發昏。」（1 章 22 節）
Sometimes we are not honest enough in our pain. Sometimes what we "know" does not fit with what we see.有時候，我們在痛苦中不夠誠實。有時候，我們所「知道」的，與我們所看見的對不上。
Yes — we know God is good and just and sovereign. But it doesn't feel like it. I know he loves me, but why all this?是的，我們知道神是良善的、公義的、掌權的。但感覺卻不是這樣。我知道祂愛我，但為甚麼會有這一切？
We are allowed to ask those uncomfortable questions. We are allowed to do it through tears. Humbly. It is not right to be angry with God. If you need encouragement in this — look at Jesus.你若需要在這事上得着鼓勵——請看耶穌。
"My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Matthew 27:46)「我的神！我的神！為甚麼離棄我？」（馬太福音 27:46）
That is godly lament. He knew why — because he was carrying our sin. But it didn't feel right. The sorrow was so overwhelming that he had to ask the question.這正是敬虔的哀慟。祂知道原因——因為祂正擔當我們的罪。但那感覺並不對勁。那憂傷實在太深，深到祂必須把那個問題問出口。
That is just where we are, this side of glory.在進入榮耀之前，我們就是活在這樣的處境中。

Good grief

合宜的哀慟

Lamentations 耶利米哀歌 2:1-22 · Bible Study Guide 聖經學習指南
"Arise, cry out in the night, as the watches of the night begin; pour out your heart like water in the presence of the Lord." (Lamentations 2:19)
「夜間，每逢交更的時候要起來呼喊，在主面前傾心如水。」（耶利米哀歌 2:19）
📖 Summary 摘要
English

Lamentations 2 sits in the smoking ruins of Jerusalem after 586 BC. The first poem ended with the city-woman sobbing into silence — but the poet has plenty left to say, because the destruction was total and the catastrophe unimaginable.


Three things fill the chapter. First, the anger of the Lord. In v.1-8 the Lord is the subject of every verb; the Babylonians are not even mentioned until v.16. God levelled his own temple, scattered his own people and ruined his own city — no mercy, no worship, no walls. It is heart-breaking, and it is richly deserved: idolatry, immorality, injustice and corruption had made the nation ripe for judgement.


Second, the grief of the prophet. Knowing the judgement is just does not make it easy to watch. The prophet's eyes fail from weeping and his heart is poured out on the ground — and not one tear is for himself. His anger falls on the false prophets who never exposed the people's sin, most likely because they never believed it themselves.


Third, the cries of the people. He will not let the survivors sit silent, bottle it up, or slip quietly into the dark. He tells them to pour out their hearts like water before the Lord. What comes out is raw — "more protest than petition, more reproach than repentance" — and yet God put it in our Bibles.


The whole chapter drives us to Christ. At the cross wrath and mercy met and the Judge was judged, so that there is now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. And the Saviour who wept over Jerusalem, and who cried "why have you forsaken me?", is the sympathetic high priest we bring our own laments to.

繁體中文

耶利米哀歌第二章，坐落在主前 586 年耶路撒冷冒煙的廢墟之中。第一首哀歌以城中婦人哭到沉默作結——但詩人仍有許多話要說，因為毀滅是徹底的，災難是無法想像的。


本章由三件事構成。第一是耶和華的怒氣。在 1 至 8 節中，耶和華是每一個動詞的主語；巴比倫人直到 16 節才被提及。神親手夷平自己的聖殿、驅散自己的子民、毀壞自己的城——沒有憐憫、沒有敬拜、沒有城牆。這令人心碎，卻是罪有應得：拜偶像、道德敗壞、不公不義、貪贓枉法，早已使這國熟透，只等審判。


第二是先知的悲痛。知道審判是公義的，並不代表看着這一切就會容易。先知眼中流淚以致失明，肝膽塗地——而沒有一滴眼淚是為自己流的。他的怒氣落在假先知身上：他們從不揭露百姓的罪，最可能的原因是他們自己根本不信。


第三是百姓的哀求。他不容許餘剩的人默默呆坐、把痛苦埋在心裏、悄然走入黑夜。他吩咐他們在主面前傾心如水。而所傾倒出來的是赤裸裸的——「與其說是祈求，不如說是抗議；與其說是悔改，不如說是責問」——神卻把它記在聖經裏。


整章都把我們推向基督。在十字架上，忿怒與憐憫相遇，審判者自己被審判，以致如今在基督耶穌裏的就不定罪了。那位曾為耶路撒冷哀哭、又曾呼喊「為甚麼離棄我」的救主，正是我們可以帶着自己的哀歌前去的、體恤人的大祭司。

💡 Key Themes 主要主題
1. God's anger is real, personal and deserved 神的怒氣是真實的、親自的、罪有應得的
English
In v.1-8 the Lord is the subject of every verb; the Babylonians only appear at v.16. This is not history running its course but God acting in judgement — and it is richly deserved, after idolatry, immorality, injustice and corruption.
繁體中文
在 1 至 8 節中，耶和華是每一個動詞的主語；巴比倫人到 16 節才出現。這不是歷史自然的演變，而是神親自施行審判——而且在拜偶像、道德敗壞、不公不義、貪贓枉法之後，這審判實在是罪有應得。
2. Wrath is not the opposite of love 忿怒並非愛的反面
English
Miroslav Volf once thought God's anger was unworthy of him — until war came to his own people. "God isn't wrathful in spite of being love. God is wrathful because God is love." God is not merely "nice"; he is good, and he will not let evil win.

3. The cross is where wrath and mercy meet 十字架是忿怒與憐憫相遇之處
English
Lamentations 2 is a preview of the "great and dreadful day of the Lord" still to come for the whole world. The only refuge from God is in God: the Judge was judged, so there is now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus.

聖潔的天父，我們讀了最難讀的一章，卻不敢轉臉不看。祢曾發怒，使黑雲遮蔽錫安城；祢親手夷平自己的聖殿、驅散自己的子民、毀壞自己的城。求祢教我們以正確的方式敬畏祢——因為落在永生神的手裏，真是可怕的。

求祢赦免我們，我們常想要一位只是「好好先生」的神。感謝祢：祢是良善的，祢就是愛；正因祢是愛，祢絕不容讓罪惡得勝。感謝祢，在十字架上，祢的忿怒與祢的憐憫相遇；審判者代替我們被審判；以致如今，那些在基督耶穌裏的就不定罪了。求祢使我們一直躲在祢裏面，得以避開祢的忿怒。

求祢賜下先知的眼淚。我們若對破碎的家庭、對孤單受虐的孩子、對關門的禮拜堂與被遺忘的福音已經麻木，求祢再一次擊碎我們的心。求祢保守我們，不因怕不受歡迎而隨波逐流或默不作聲；求祢使我們在所說所教的事上忠心。

當我們無法明白眼前的一切，求祢幫助我們不要默默呆坐，也不要滑向絕望，而是在祢面前傾心如水——誠實地、謙卑地、含着眼淚地傾倒。感謝祢，我們的救主曾為一座城哀哭，也曾在十字架上呼喊；直到今日，祂仍體恤我們。我們把自己的罪、自己的困惑、自己的哀傷都帶到祢面前，等候那每早晨都是新的慈愛。奉耶穌的名求，阿們。
