Bilingual Sermon 中英雙語講道
The Anchor for our Soul 靈魂的錨
(Hebrews 希伯來書 6:13-20)

Intro: How certain is your hope?引言：你的盼望有多確定？
Let me ask you this morning: if you are a believer in the Lord Jesus — how confident are you that, in the words of Hebrews 4, eternal Sabbath-rest is coming?今早讓我問你：如果你是信靠主耶穌的人——按希伯來書第 4 章的說法，你有多確定那永遠的安息正要來臨？
That is God’s promise for all those who’ve had their sins taken away by our High Priest, the Lord Jesus. And it’s a promise we are still waiting to see fulfilled.這是神給每一個藉著我們的大祭司主耶穌、罪得赦免之人的應許；而這應許，我們至今仍在等候它完全實現。
How confident are you that, no matter how difficult things might be at present (and for many of you life is hard at the moment), this future of joy-filled eternal rest is guaranteed?無論眼前的處境多麼艱難（你們當中許多人此刻的生活實在不容易），你有多確定這充滿喜樂、永遠安息的將來是必然成就的？
In other words: how certain is your hope? On a scale of 1 to 10.換句話說：你的盼望有多確定？若以一至十分來衡量呢？
I ask that question because hope for the future greatly affects how we’re doing in the present.我問這個問題，是因為對將來的盼望，會深深影響我們今天的光景。
In the words of v.19: "hope is an anchor for the soul". It keeps us steady and steadfast.正如第 19 節所說：「這指望如同靈魂的錨」。它使我們穩固、堅定不移。
Tim Keller tells a true story about two men going to prison. Just before they went in, one discovered that the rest of his family were dead; the other learned that his family were alive and waiting for him. In the first couple of years the first man simply wasted away, curled up and died. But the other endured, stayed strong, and walked out a free man ten years later.提姆·凱勒（Tim Keller）講過一個真實的故事：有兩個人將要入獄。臨入獄前，一個人得知家人全都離世了，另一個人卻得知家人都還活著、正等候他歸來。頭兩年間，第一個人日漸消沉，蜷縮一角，最終死去；另一個人卻撐了過來，保持剛強，十年後以自由之身步出監牢。
The point is this: both men experienced the same circumstances, but they responded differently based on their hope (or lack of hope) for the future.重點在於：兩人身處相同的境況，反應卻截然不同——分別就在於他們對將來有沒有盼望。
The writer has been urging and warning his listeners to keep going with Jesus. But he’s also confident they’ll make it (vv.9-12). Their hope is certain.作者一直在勸勉、也警戒讀者要持守、與耶穌一同走下去；但他同時深信他們必能到達終點（9 至 12 節）。他們的盼望是確定的。
Did you notice — God wants you and me to be certain that eternal rest (ch.4) is coming. And he wants us certain because he wants us to be greatly encouraged as we wait. If we’re not certain, we’re more likely to drift and go cold.你有沒有留意——神要你和我確定那永遠的安息（第 4 章）必定來到；祂要我們確定，是因為祂要我們在等候之中大得勉勵。我們若不確定，就更容易隨流飄去、心漸冷淡。
The question is: what makes it so certain? In short — the promises of God, and the God of those promises.問題是：是甚麼使這盼望如此確定？簡言之——是神的應許，以及那位賜下應許的神。
If we’re doubting whether that future is on the cards — look up. We need to hear that this morning, because the world around us, if we’re not careful, can train us to look elsewhere to be certain of a brighter future.我們若懷疑那將來是否真會臨到——就當舉目向上。今早我們實在需要聽見這話，因為若不謹慎，周遭的世界會慢慢訓練我們，把確據寄託在別處，以求一個更美好的明天。
The world also wants to believe in a better future — but where does it tell us to put our confidence?這世界同樣渴望相信有更美好的將來——但它叫我們把信心放在哪裡呢？
Look within: from adverts, to what our kids are taught at school, to self-help books, the message is — you can be the reason for a better tomorrow. Hope is found in you; just look inside and go get it. The trouble is: we don’t always act in our best interests; we don’t know what we want or what will guarantee a better future; and we’re often at the mercy of bigger forces.向內看：從廣告、到學校所教導孩子的、到坊間的勵志書籍，傳遞的信息都是——你自己就能成就更美好的明天；盼望在乎你，只要向內尋找、奮力爭取便是。問題是：我們未必常常為自己的真正益處而行；我們其實不清楚自己想要甚麼、也不知道甚麼能保證更好的將來；而且我們往往受制於更大的力量。
Look to technology: AI is coming to save the day and usher in a better world. AI does bring improvements, yes — but the same AI can also be used for incredible destruction.向科技看：人工智能彷彿要來拯救一切、開創更美好的世界。誠然，人工智能帶來不少進步——但同一樣的人工智能，也能被用來造成驚人的破壞。
Look to governments: more than ever there’s a belief that government should sort everything out. But governments can be corrupt, or incapable; government isn’t even sure itself what ‘better’ means or looks like.向政府看：如今人們比以往更相信，政府理應把一切都安排妥當。但政府可以是腐敗的，也可以是無能的；甚至連政府自己也說不清「更好」究竟是甚麼、是甚麼模樣。
Hebrews 6 holds out a far more certain hope: the believer in the Lord Jesus can be certain of eternal rest by looking outside of ourselves — outside of the world even — to its Maker and Sustainer, to the One who makes promises that can be kept, and who cannot do otherwise.希伯來書第 6 章卻擺出一個確定得多的盼望：信靠主耶穌的人能確知那永遠的安息，乃是藉著向自身以外去看——甚至向這世界以外去看——仰望那位創造並托住萬有的主，仰望那位所應許的必能成就、且斷不能失信的神。
Where do we look? Three areas. Three points this morning.我們當往哪裡看？有三方面。今早分三點來看。
………………………………………………
1. The promises of God (vv.13-16)一、神的應許（13 至 16 節）
Trust is at a low ebb at the moment — particularly in leaders and institutions.如今人與人之間的信任正處於低谷——尤其是對領袖和機構的信任。
Woodrow Wilson’s campaign slogan was ‘he kept us out of war’. Thirty-four days after being elected, America entered the First World War.伍德羅·威爾遜（Woodrow Wilson）競選的口號是「他使我們免於戰爭」。當選後三十四天，美國就加入了第一次世界大戰。
Tuition fees: ‘we won’t raise them,’ said Nick Clegg. Then he joined a coalition government — and fees rose by about six thousand pounds a year.再說大學學費：尼克·克萊格（Nick Clegg）說過「我們不會加學費」。後來他加入聯合政府——學費卻每年漲了大約六千英鎊。
It can be hard to trust what is promised, given people’s track record.看看人過往的「往績」，我們實在很難全然相信別人所應許的。
I’ve promised my kids things — we’ll do this this weekend, we’ll go there. But maybe I get ill, or something urgent comes up, and we can’t do what I promised.我也曾向兒女應許過：這個週末我們要做這件事、要去那個地方。可是也許我病了，或臨時有要事，結果就無法兌現所應許的。
But God doesn’t want us to be cynical or to doubt what he can do.然而神不願我們變得憤世嫉俗，或懷疑祂的能力。
The writer draws our attention to Abraham. Of anyone, Abraham had plenty of reasons to doubt God’s promises — for they were outrageous promises.作者把我們的目光引向亞伯拉罕。若論誰最有理由懷疑神的應許，亞伯拉罕當數其一——因為神給他的應許實在大得驚人。
Abraham was just some bloke — a seventy-five-year-old man — when God came to him and promised to make him into a great nation, and that from that nation would come someone who would change the world; who would bless the world.亞伯拉罕本是個平凡人——一個七十五歲的老人——神卻臨到他，應許要使他成為大國，並且從這國要出一位改變世界、賜福萬國的後裔。
And Abraham had to do a lot of waiting. While he waited, life was not at all easy: he faced famine, exile, war, an ageing body.而亞伯拉罕必須等候許久。等候之中，生活絕不輕省：他經歷饑荒、漂流寄居、爭戰，身體也日漸衰老。
But God’s promises were fulfilled. Not completely in Abraham’s lifetime, but he saw the beginning of their fulfilment. A son was born — and from his offspring came a nation, and from that nation came Jesus, and from Jesus came his church and millions upon millions saved across the earth. And one day Jesus’ new-creation kingdom will cover the earth.但神的應許一一應驗了。雖未在亞伯拉罕一生之內完全成就，他卻看見了應驗的起頭。一個兒子出生了——從他的後裔出了一個民族，從這民族出了耶穌，從耶穌出了祂的教會，並普世千千萬萬蒙拯救的人。將來有一天，耶穌新創造的國度必充滿全地。
The one steady constant throughout Abraham’s life, through the history of the world, down to our own lives and beyond, is the promises of God being fulfilled.縱觀亞伯拉罕的一生、世界的歷史，以至我們自己的人生及至永遠，那唯一穩固不變的常數，就是神的應許不斷被成就。
But the writer doesn’t just want us to look back on God’s track record. He zeroes in on an oath God made to Abraham in Genesis 22. Why?但作者並非只要我們回顧神的「往績」。他特別聚焦於神在創世記第 22 章向亞伯拉罕所起的誓。為甚麼呢？
Again, because God wants Abraham to be greatly encouraged. God is utterly committed to delivering his promises.同樣，是因為神要亞伯拉罕大得勉勵。神是全然委身、必要成就祂應許的神。
We still understand today the weight of swearing an oath. We usually swear by a greater authority to give weight to what we’re saying.時至今日，我們仍明白起誓的份量。人通常指著一個更高的權柄起誓，好叫所說的話更有份量。
My friends at school, when they wanted me to be sure of something, would say: ‘I swear on my mother’s life.’我讀書時的同學，若要我相信某件事，便會說：「我以我母親的性命起誓。」
It’s a huge part of how our judicial system works: oaths give us confidence that justice is being done. A witness declares, ‘I swear that the evidence I shall give shall be the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth.’起誓也是司法制度運作的重要一環：誓言讓我們相信公義正在伸張。證人會宣誓說：「我謹此起誓，我所作的證供乃是真話，全部真話，除真話以外別無其他。」
It’s why perjury is punished so severely. Perjury is the crime of intentionally lying after swearing an oath. Oaths are meant to guarantee that what is said is true; if you’ve lied, you can be jailed for years.這也是為何作偽證會受到如此嚴厲的懲罰。偽證罪，就是在起誓之後仍故意說謊。誓言本是要擔保所說的是真的；你若說了謊，便可能要坐牢多年。
Now, with God there is no greater authority by which he can swear — so he swears by himself. If he’d sworn by anything else, that oath could be broken.至於神，再沒有比祂更大的權柄可供祂指著起誓——所以祂指著自己起誓。祂若指著別的起誓，那誓言便有可能落空。
If God had sworn by heaven or by earth — well, heaven and earth could pass away, and so his oath might not come about.倘若神指著天或地起誓——天地卻會廢去，那麼祂的誓言便有可能不能成就。
When God swears by himself, he’s saying: this promise is as likely to fail as it is likely that God himself could cease to exist. In other words — impossible to fail!當神指著自己起誓，祂其實是說：這應許落空的可能，等於神自己會止息存在的可能。換句話說——絕無落空之可能！
To be clear: God doesn’t need to swear an oath. This is not for his benefit — he does it for ours. Read v.17.要說清楚：神並不需要起誓。這不是為祂自己的益處——祂這樣做，是為了我們的益處。請讀第 17 節。
"God did this so that, by two unchangeable things in which it is impossible for God to lie, we who have fled to take hold of the hope set before us may be greatly encouraged." (vv.17-18)「照樣，神願意為那承受應許的人格外顯明他的旨意是不更改的，就起誓為證。藉這兩件不更改的事，神決不能說謊，好叫我們這逃往避難所、持定擺在我們前頭指望的人可以大得勉勵。」（17 至 18 節）
How gracious is God? Do you see his heart for his people? He wants us to know that he does come through on his promises — because he wants us to stand firm in the present.神是何等有恩典！你看見祂愛祂子民的心嗎？祂要我們確知祂必兌現應許——因為祂要我們在現今的日子站立得穩。
It can be hard, in a world of war, cancer and difficult relationships, to believe eternal rest is coming — that our Saviour will return and bring us home.在這充滿戰爭、癌症與難處關係的世界裡，要相信永遠的安息正在來臨——相信我們的救主必要再來、接我們回家——確實不容易。
But God swore an oath for Abraham, and that same oath applies to us, for we are the heirs of Abraham.但神曾為亞伯拉罕起誓，而那同一個誓言也臨到我們，因為我們正是亞伯拉罕的後嗣。
God wants us to feel secure. He wants us to have that anchor. That promise of eternal rest for Jesus’ followers is going to happen. So be encouraged as we wait.神要我們有穩妥之感。祂要我們擁有那靈魂的錨。那給跟隨耶穌之人、永遠安息的應許必定成就。所以，當我們等候時，要大得勉勵。

2. The God of the promises (vv.17-18)二、賜應許的神（17 至 18 節）
We have certainty not just because of God’s promises, but because of the God who makes those promises.我們之所以有確據，不單因為神的應許，更因為那位賜下應許的神。
God’s promise and God’s oath show that we need not doubt that our future as God’s people is secure. But the passage gives us even more reason to be sure: God himself — his character.神的應許與神的誓言顯明：我們作為神子民，前途穩妥，無須懷疑。但這段經文還給我們更大的確據：就是神自己——祂的本性。
Verse 17 speaks of "the unchanging nature of God’s purpose" — telling us something about who God is. He is unchanging. And that is good news.第 17 節提到「神的旨意是不更改的」——這向我們說明神是怎樣的一位。祂是不改變的。而這正是好消息。
Our world is constantly changing, isn’t it? New AI models become outdated after a month or two; updates never stop.我們的世界不是時刻在變嗎？新的人工智能模型一兩個月便過時了；更新從不止息。
Language changes — trying to keep up with modern slang is hard.語言在變——要跟上潮流用語實在不易。
Even a trivial example: paying for parking has changed — coins, then card, now neither: you have to download an app to pay. And that’ll probably change again.就連停車繳費這種小事也在變——先是投硬幣，後來用咭，如今兩者都不行：你得下載應用程式才能付款。而這大概很快又會再變。
Life is changing. Places are changing. The way we do things is changing. Laws are changing. U-turns left, right and centre.人生在變，地方在變，做事的方式在變，法例也在變，政策反覆無常、處處掉頭。
Change can be good — but change can also make us less trusting of things.改變可以是好的——但改變也會叫我們對事物更難以信任。
There’s a comfort in things that don’t change. It’s why I love the sea and the mountains: I go back to the beach I went to as a child, and the rocks are still the same. The mountains don’t change.在那些不變的事物中，有一種安慰。這正是我喜愛大海與群山的原因：我重回兒時去過的海灘，那些岩石依然如故；群山也不改變。
God has an unchanging nature. What comfort for us — especially since he is a God who makes promises. His promises cannot change, because he cannot change.神有不改變的本性。這對我們是何等的安慰——尤其因為祂是一位賜應許的神。祂的應許不能改變，因為祂自己不能改變。
And v.18 says God cannot lie. What he has promised and sworn with an oath — two unchangeable things — will happen.第 18 節又說，神決不能說謊。祂所應許、又起誓為證的——這兩件不更改的事——必定成就。
Whatever God says, reality has to conform to what he has said. It is going to happen.凡神所說的，現實都必須與祂的話相符。事情必要成就。
There’s no hidden small print or terms and conditions that undermine his promises. God isn’t a phone or broadband provider trying to be sneaky.祂的應許背後並沒有暗藏的細則或條款去削弱它。神不像那些耍手段、暗藏條件的電話或寬頻供應商。
What guarantees the promise — and so what anchors our hope — is not us, but who our promise-maker is. He is unchanging and cannot lie.擔保這應許的——也就是那穩住我們盼望之錨的——不是我們，而是那位賜應許者的身分。祂不改變，也不能說謊。
That promise of eternal rest for Jesus’ followers is going to happen. So be encouraged as we wait.那給跟隨耶穌之人、永遠安息的應許必定成就。所以，當我們等候時，要大得勉勵。

3. Jesus is already there (vv.19-20)三、耶穌已經在那裡（19 至 20 節）
Something can seem impossible — until you realise someone has actually done it.有些事看似不可能——直到你發現，原來已經有人做到了。
Swimming the English Channel, from England to France, was thought impossible a hundred and fifty years ago. Then Matthew Webb did it unaided. Now thousands do it.一百五十年前，人們以為橫渡英倫海峽、從英格蘭游到法國是不可能的。後來馬修·韋伯（Matthew Webb）在無人輔助下做到了。如今已有數以千計的人做到。
Notice v.19: our hope of eternal rest is an anchor — this hope is as firm and secure as God himself.請留意第 19 節：我們對永遠安息的盼望是一個錨——這盼望像神自己一樣堅固牢靠。
But notice, too, that our hope is living and breathing. For we have a living, breathing representative already in God’s presence — a person who has gone where we hope to go, who has gone into the inner sanctuary behind the curtain, the holiest part of the Temple, where God’s presence was.但也請留意，我們的盼望是有生命、活活潑潑的。因為我們已經有一位有生命、活活潑潑的代表進到神面前——祂已經去到我們所盼望要去的地方，進入了幔子內的至聖所，就是聖殿中最聖潔、有神同在之處。
In other words, Jesus right now enjoys eternal rest with God. He is there, and so millions more can follow.換句話說，耶穌此刻正與神一同享受永遠的安息。祂在那裡，所以還有千千萬萬的人能跟隨而至。
In fact, he’s making preparations for our arrival even now:事實上，祂此刻正為我們的到來作預備：
"My Father’s house has many rooms; if that were not so, would I have told you that I am going there to prepare a place for you? And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come back and take you to be with me, that you also may be where I am. You know the way to the place where I am going." (John 14:2-4)「在我父的家裡有許多住處；若是沒有，我就早已告訴你們了。我去原是為你們預備地方去。我若去為你們預備了地方，就必再來接你們到我那裡去，我在哪裡，叫你們也在那裡。我往哪裡去，你們知道；那條路，你們也知道。」（約翰福音 14 章 2 至 4 節）
We have complete assurance that God’s promise will be carried out, because our representative is there already. That eternal rest we crave is being enjoyed even now by the very One who gets us there.我們有十足的把握，神的應許必要成就，因為我們的代表已經在那裡了。我們所渴慕的那永遠安息，此刻正被那位帶領我們進入安息的主所享受著。
The writer will have much more to say about Jesus as a priest in the order of Melchizedek — he was getting there back in ch.5 before he paused. More on that next time.關於耶穌作那照著麥基洗德等次的祭司，作者還有許多要說——他在第 5 章本已說到那裡，卻暫且停下。這個題目，下次再講。

Conclusion: We are in the waiting room結語：我們身在等候室
Friends, if you’re feeling uncertain about the future, God doesn’t want that for you. Uncertainty can lead to drifting.弟兄姊妹，你若對將來感到不確定，神並不願你如此。不確定會叫人隨流飄去。
One of the best ways to avoid drift is to have certain hope. And that’s exactly what God gives us.防止飄流最好的方法之一，就是擁有確定的盼望。而這正是神所賜給我們的。

The Anchor for our Soul

靈魂的錨

Hebrews 希伯來書 6:13-20 · Bible Study Guide 聖經學習指南
"We have this hope as an anchor for the soul, firm and secure." · 「我們有這指望，如同靈魂的錨，又堅固又牢靠。」(Hebrews 希伯來書 6 章 19 節)
📖 Summary 摘要
English
Hebrews 6:13-20 asks a searching question: how certain is your hope? Because the writer knows that hope for the future shapes how we cope in the present, like Tim Keller’s two prisoners who faced the same cell but lived very differently. The world tells us to find a brighter future by looking within (self-help), to technology (AI), or to governments — but each of these can fail us. Hebrews points us outside ourselves, to God, and gives three anchors for our soul. First, the promises of God (vv.13-16): unlike Woodrow Wilson or Nick Clegg, God keeps his word. He proved it to Abraham over a lifetime of waiting, and then sealed it with an oath sworn by himself — for no greater authority exists. Second, the God of the promises (vv.17-18): in a world of relentless change, God is unchanging and cannot lie; what anchors our hope is not us but the character of the promise-maker. Third, Jesus is already there (vv.19-20): our hope is living, because our representative has gone behind the curtain into God’s presence, enjoys that rest now, and is preparing a place for us (John 14). So we wait in the ‘waiting room’ — not anxiously, but with great encouragement, because our hope is sure, our hope is there already, and we will be there too.
繁體中文
希伯來書 6 章 13 至 20 節提出一個叩問人心的問題：你的盼望有多確定？因為作者深知，對將來的盼望會塑造我們今天如何面對處境——正如提姆·凱勒所講的兩個囚犯，身處同一牢房，活出來的卻截然不同。世界叫我們向內尋找更美好的將來（勵志自助）、寄望於科技（人工智能）、或仰賴政府——但這三者都可能叫我們失望。希伯來書卻把我們的目光引向自身以外，仰望神，並賜下三個靈魂之錨。第一，神的應許（13 至 16 節）：神不像威爾遜或克萊格，祂必守自己的話。祂用亞伯拉罕一生的等候印證了這事，又以指著自己所起的誓來加以確立——因為再沒有比祂更大的權柄。第二，賜應許的神（17 至 18 節）：在這變幻不息的世界裡，神是不改變、不能說謊的；穩住我們盼望的，不是我們，而是那賜應許者的本性。第三，耶穌已經在那裡（19 至 20 節）：我們的盼望是活的，因為我們的代表已經進入幔內、到了神面前，此刻正享受那安息，並為我們預備地方（約翰福音 14 章）。因此我們身在「等候室」中等候——不是焦慮，而是大得勉勵，因為我們的盼望確實、已經在那裡，而我們也必要到那裡去。
