📖 Summary / Di Shot Vashon
English
Hebrews 7:1-10 asks a surprising question: Melchizedek who? He appears in only three verses of Genesis 14 and one verse of Psalm 110, yet the writer treats him as vital — because Melchizedek shines a light not on himself but on the greatness of the Lord Jesus. The whole letter argues that Jesus is better, and one reason is that he is a priest ‘in the order of Melchizedek’, not of Levi. The sermon draws out three ways Melchizedek’s greatness points to Christ. First, Melchizedek resembles Jesus (vv.2-3): his name means ‘king of righteousness’, he is king of Salem (peace), and Genesis is silent about his genealogy, birth and death — portraying a priest who ‘remains forever’. Jesus’ priesthood really is eternal, so access to God, grace and mercy are ours forever. Second, Melchizedek trumps Abraham and the Levites (vv.4-10): Abraham, the great patriarch, pays him a tithe and is blessed by him — and the lesser is blessed by the greater. What the readers were tempted to return to (the Levitical system) is inferior. Third, Melchizedek combines two offices (v.1): he is both king and priest — something forbidden in the Old Testament — which points to Jesus alone, who opens access to God AND guarantees final victory. Infinite mercy with infinite justice; infinite grace with infinite power. So don’t turn away from Jesus — look to him for peace, righteousness and security, and read the Scriptures to keep beholding his greatness.

🌱 Application / Aw fo Yuz Am na Layf
English
This passage is ultimately pastoral: it wants weary, tempted believers to keep going with Jesus. Three concrete steps. (1) Name where you’re drifting. Like a QWERTY keyboard we return to out of habit, we drift back to ‘familiar but lesser’ sources of security — performance, approval, control, old comforts. Identify one this week and consciously turn back to Jesus. (2) Rest in an unchanging priest. When life feels chaotic and you’ve let God down again, remember that access, grace and mercy are forever open through a priest who remains forever. Bring your failure to the throne of grace rather than hiding from it. (3) Read to behold him. Take even ‘minor’ passages (like three verses about Melchizedek) and ask, ‘how does this show me Jesus’ greatness?’ Let Scripture keep his superiority in view, so that seeing his greatness, you stay with him.

🙏 Prayer / Prea
English

In his name we pray, Amen.
