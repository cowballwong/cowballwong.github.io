Bilingual Sermon 中英雙語講道
A groaning city 一座呻吟的城
(Lamentations 1:1-22 耶利米哀歌 1:1-22)

A groaning city一座呻吟的城
Big idea: this once great city is now disgraced, despised and deserted. It is heartbreaking to watch — but they have only themselves to blame.中心思想：這座曾經偉大的城，如今蒙羞、被藐視、被遺棄。看見這光景固然叫人心碎，但這一切都是他們自招的。
………………………………
The word ‘tragedy’ is overused today. But if it belongs anywhere, surely it belongs here.「悲劇」這個詞今天被用得太濫。但若說有甚麼真配得上這兩個字，這裏必然是。
What happened to Jerusalem in 587 BC is a thing of such horror that we would rather turn away than stop, look and listen. Yet this morning we are going to stop, and look, and listen.主前 587 年臨到耶路撒冷的事，恐怖得叫人寧願別過臉去，也不願停下來看、停下來聽。但今早我們正要停下來，去看，去聽。
Because although this is a tough read, it is here because we need this book. Like no other book in the Bible it shows us the severity of sin. But it also shows us how to give voice to our pain — as the Psalms do — and where to find hope, even as it points beyond itself to the rest of Scripture. Ultimately it points to Jesus.因為這卷書雖然讀來沉重，它之所以被收在聖經裏，正是因為我們需要它。沒有一卷書像它這樣，把罪的嚴重性擺在我們眼前；但它同時教我們怎樣把痛苦說出口（正如詩篇一樣），也指示我們在哪裏可以尋得盼望——它更越過自身，指向聖經其餘各卷，最終指向耶穌基督。
………………………………
Who wrote it?作者是誰？
Clearly an eye-witness, and a poet. Possibly the prophet Jeremiah — which is why the two books sit together in modern Bibles.作者顯然是一位親歷其境的目擊者，也是一位詩人。很可能就是先知耶利米——這也是現代聖經把兩卷書排在一起的緣故。
Jeremiah had desperately tried to prevent this. Again and again he called an increasingly wayward and wicked people back to God. They would not listen. And so he was left sitting among the smoking ruins of what was once a great city.耶利米曾竭力想攔阻這事。他一次又一次呼喚那日益悖逆、日益敗壞的百姓歸向神，他們卻不肯聽。到頭來，他只能坐在那曾經偉大的城的餘燼廢墟之中。
………………………………
How the book is put together這卷書的結構
It is highly structured. Five poems, arranged almost symmetrically. Poems 1, 2, 4 and 5 have twenty-two parts each — which is why our English Bibles have twenty-two verses. The high point at the centre, chapter 3, has sixty-six.全書結構嚴謹：五首詩，幾乎對稱地排列。第一、二、四、五首各有二十二節——所以我們的聖經每章都是二十二節。位於正中央的高峰，就是第三章，有六十六節。
Twenty-two is not random: it is the number of letters in the Hebrew alphabet. In poems 1 to 4, each of the twenty-two parts begins with a different letter — an A-to-Z of suffering and lament. And although there is much repetition, each poem makes its own particular contribution.二十二不是隨意的數目，那是希伯來文字母的數目。第一至第四首詩，二十二節各以不同的字母開頭——是一部苦難與哀歌的「從頭到尾」。雖然當中有許多重複，每一首詩仍各有其獨特的貢獻。
………………………………
1. A brutal reversal一、徹底的翻轉
Oh how the mighty have fallen. Read verses 1, 4 and 6-7. In this first part the poet speaks as an observer, and what he sees is horrifying.曾經何等強盛，如今何等傾覆。請讀 1 節、4 節、6 至 7 節。在第一部分，詩人是以旁觀者的身分說話，而他所看見的，實在可怖。
In 587 BC the armies of Babylon breached the walls of Jerusalem and poured into the city — looting, setting fires, razing it to the ground. But that was only the final act, after a decade of brutal oppression and months of a bitter, devastating siege (2 Kings 25), with widespread starvation.主前 587 年，巴比倫的軍隊攻破耶路撒冷的城牆，湧進城中——搶掠、縱火、把全城夷為平地。但那不過是最後一幕，之前是十年的殘酷壓迫，以及數月苦不堪言的圍城（列王紀下 25 章），城中處處是飢荒。
Once the apple of God’s eye and the jewel of the whole earth — now a heap of rubble with vultures circling overhead.這城曾是神眼中的瞳人，是全地的珍寶——如今卻是一堆瓦礫，禿鷹在上空盤旋。
Zion is another name for the city, drawing particular attention to it as the city of God, where his temple presence used to be. And Zion here is a woman: ravaged by the enemy, now in the deepest depths of mourning and grief. All her best men are either killed or taken captive. It is as if history has gone into reverse.「錫安」是這城的另一個名字，特別強調它是「神的城」，是祂的殿、祂的同在曾經所在之處。而這裏的錫安是一個婦人：被仇敵蹂躪，如今陷在最深的哀慟與悲傷之中。她最好的男丁不是被殺，就是被擄。彷彿歷史整個倒轉過來。
If one word pulls it all together, it is this: groan. It appears five times (v.4, 8, 11, 21, 22). The poem opens with a city full of people (v.1). It closes with a city full of groaning (v.22).若有一個字把這一切串連起來，就是「歎息」。它出現了五次（4、8、11、21、22 節）。詩的開頭，是一座人煙稠密的城（1 節）；詩的結尾，是一座滿了歎息的城（22 節）。
The reminder is this: things can change very quickly — for better or for worse — whatever today looks like.這提醒我們：無論今天看來如何，情況可以很快就改變——可以變好，也可以變壞。
Seneca, the Stoic philosopher of the first century and personal tutor to the Emperor Nero, put it like this: ‘There is but one day between a great city and no city.’ How many nations, and how many people, have learnt that the hard way.主後一世紀的斯多噶派哲學家塞內卡，是尼祿皇帝的私人教師，他這樣說：「一座大城與化為烏有之間，只隔著一日。」多少國家、多少人，都是用最慘痛的方式學會這一課。
………………………………
2. A crippling isolation二、令人癱瘓的孤單
One phrase appears six times in this chapter — and never again in the book. Four times from the poet, twice from Lady Zion herself when she speaks, mostly in the second half: there is no one to comfort her.有一句話在本章出現了六次，而全書其餘部分再沒有出現過。四次出自詩人，兩次出自錫安自己的口——她主要在後半段說話：無人安慰她。
The point is not merely that things are very bad. It is that no one cares. Lady Zion is a bereaved widow: lonely, vulnerable, abandoned to suffer her fate alone. And she is doubly deserted.重點不單是處境極其惡劣，而是沒有人理會。錫安是一個守寡的婦人：孤單、脆弱、被撇下獨自承受自己的命運。而她是被雙重地離棄。
First, her friends have walked away. Read verse 2, and verse 21. Just when she most needed someone to sit with her and hold her — there was no one. ‘I called to my allies but they betrayed me’ (v.19). Read verse 16.第一，她的朋友離她而去。請讀 2 節和 21 節。正當她最需要有人陪伴、有人扶持的時候——一個也沒有。「我招呼我所親愛的，他們卻愚弄我」（19 節）。請讀 16 節。
Second, it feels as though the Lord has walked away too. Read verse 11 and verse 20. Four times she cries out to the Lord to notice, to see, to look. Nothing comes back. No word of reassurance. In fact nowhere in the whole of Lamentations does God say anything. He is silent throughout. There is no one to comfort.第二，連主彷彿也離她而去。請讀 11 節和 20 節。她四次向主呼求，求祂垂顧、求祂觀看、求祂看見。卻毫無回音，沒有一句安慰的話。事實上，整卷耶利米哀歌裏，神一句話也沒有說過。祂由頭到尾都是沉默的。無人安慰。
………………………………
What this teaches us這給我們的功課
Our suffering is so much worse alone. Hard times are so much harder when there is no one to comfort us. We live in an epidemic of loneliness — largely through the breakdown of the family — so it is no wonder that so many are in despair.苦難若是獨自承受，就沉重得多。艱難的日子，若沒有人安慰，就更加難捱。我們身處一場孤單的疫症之中——很大程度上源於家庭的破碎——難怪有這麼多人陷在絕望裏。
Which is exactly where we can make such a difference. The family of God can be so powerful here. ‘Mourn with those who mourn’ (Romans 12:15). We often cannot make the problem go away — but we can be there. To hold, to listen, to share life, simply to be present. That can be enormously powerful.而這正是我們可以帶來極大分別的地方。神的家在這裏可以何等有力。「與哀哭的人要同哭」（羅馬書 12:15）。我們往往不能使問題消失——但我們可以在場。可以扶持、可以聆聽、可以一同生活、單單是陪伴。這已經可以極其有力。
An illustration from my own life: my sabbatical in 2025. I was in a dark place. Two things sustained, refreshed and strengthened me. First, receiving ministry — I was running on fumes, and I simply needed to attend church and let someone else do all the worrying. Second, rediscovering the grace of fellowship — visiting friends just to be with them, with no pressure. We can be that to one another.這裏有一個我自己的例子：2025 年我的安息年假。那時我身處幽暗之中。有兩樣東西托住我、使我重新得力。第一，接受服侍——當時我已經燃盡，我只需要單純地回到教會，讓別人去擔憂。第二，重新發現團契的恩典——去探望朋友，只為與他們同在，沒有任何壓力。我們也可以這樣彼此相待。
………………………………
3. An avoidable ruin三、本可避免的毀滅
Now the elephant in the room: the reason for heaven’s silence in this case. It is not that God does not care. It is that they brought this upon themselves.現在要面對那個沒有人願意提的問題：在這件事上，天為甚麼沉默。並不是因為神不理會，而是因為這是他們自招的。
The Lord had pleaded with them to abandon their idolatry and their wicked ways — including rampant corruption and injustice. They refused to listen. And now it is too late. They probably thought the Lord was bluffing, or took his patience for indifference. Now they know better. Read verses 5, 8 and 14-15.主曾一再懇求他們離棄拜偶像與一切惡行——包括那橫行的貪腐與不義。他們拒絕聆聽，如今為時已晚。他們大概以為主只是嚇唬他們，又或者把祂的忍耐當作漠不關心。如今他們知道了。請讀 5 節、8 節、14 至 15 節。
‘Behind the hammer of Babylon was the hand of God.’ (Chris Wright)「在巴比倫的鎚子背後，是神的手。」（Chris Wright）
………………………………
There is more going on with those ‘friends’ who betrayed her than we might at first realise — and with her ‘lovers’ too. Read verse 2. The word itself is not necessarily negative, but it is here.那些出賣她的「朋友」，背後的意思比我們初讀時所想的更深——她的「情人」也是一樣。請讀 2 節。這個字本身未必是負面的，但在這裏是。
They had got into bed with the peoples and nations around them — Moab, Ammon, Edom. The Lord had said no, mostly for their own good. They would not listen. Rather than trust the Lord to keep them safe and to provide, they formed alliances. And here they are. It is a kind of poetic justice: left high and dry by the very lovers they should never have had.他們與周圍的民族與列國同床共枕——摩押、亞捫、以東。主曾說不可，而且多半是為他們自己好。他們不肯聽。他們不肯信靠主的保護與供應，反而去結盟。結果就是眼前這光景。這帶著一種因果的公道：被那些本來就不該有的情人，撇在一旁，孤立無援。
Why did they not listen? Read verse 9. They thought it would never happen. It has.他們為甚麼不肯聽？請讀 9 節。他們以為這種事永遠不會發生。如今發生了。
………………………………
What this teaches us這給我們的功課
In a world where God is just, sin must have consequences. So often we do not think it will — and it does. We do not consider the future. But we must.在一個有公義之神的世界裏，罪必然有後果。我們常常以為不會——結果卻會。我們不去思想將來，但我們必須思想。
How often we make a shipwreck of our lives. The one-night stand that ends in a pregnancy. The abortion that leaves deep psychological scars. The gambling addiction that costs the home. The problem drinking that becomes chronic ill health. The festering rage that ends in a prison sentence. Please — stop and think.我們何等常常把自己的人生弄成一場船難。一夜情，結果是懷孕。墮胎，留下深刻的心理創傷。賭癮，賠上一頭家。酗酒，換來長期病患。積壓的怒氣，最終換來牢獄之災。求你——停一停，想一想。
And even if we get away with it in this life, we will not in the next. This is a vision of hell — so perhaps it is closer to home than we think. Jesus spoke of the place ‘where their worm does not die and the fire is not quenched’ (Mark 9:48). That is not merely a picture of hell; it is hell itself. He is not bluffing. So the question is whether we are listening now.即使今生僥倖逃過，來生也逃不過。這是地獄的圖畫——所以它離我們，或許比我們所想的更近。耶穌曾提到那地方，「在那裏，蟲是不死的，火是不滅的」（馬可福音 9:48）。那不單是地獄的比喻，那就是地獄本身。祂不是在嚇唬人。所以問題是：我們現在有沒有在聽。
Realise it now. If we treat the God of the universe with contempt, if we are determined to deny and defy our Maker to the end — this will be us. And yet it need not be.現在就要看清楚。我們若藐視這位宇宙的神，若定意到底否認祂、抗拒這位造我們的主——這將會是我們的結局。然而，這並非必然。
………………………………
Back to Jesus, by another route從另一條路回到耶穌
There are no direct quotations from Lamentations in the New Testament, but there are several allusions. The most striking and forceful is our Saviour’s own overwhelming suffering — his hell. Read verse 12.新約沒有直接引用耶利米哀歌，卻有幾處明顯的呼應。當中最觸目、最有力的，就是我們救主自己那排山倒海的受苦——祂的地獄。請讀 12 節。
Six hundred years later, another scene of horror took place in this very same place — but this time to one man alone. There were many who ‘passed by’ (Matthew 27:38-40; Mark 15:29-30). Some barely noticed. Some shook their heads. Others laughed and joined in.六百年後，同樣的地方發生另一幕可怖的景象——但這一次，臨到的只是一個人。有許多「從那裏經過的人」（馬太福音 27:38-40；馬可福音 15:29-30）。有些幾乎看也不看，有些搖著頭，也有些譏笑並且加入辱罵。
He too was betrayed by his friends. He too was stripped naked by his enemies. He too was all alone. As he hung there, baking in the noonday sun, his life draining away, he cried out — and there was no one to comfort him, and heaven was silent. Never was there sorrow like this.祂也被朋友出賣。祂也被仇敵剝去衣裳。祂也是孤身一人。當祂懸在那裏，在正午的烈日下被曬烤，生命一點一點流逝，祂呼喊——卻無人安慰，天也沉默。從來沒有像這樣的憂傷。
‘Jerusalem’s destruction and exile is Jesus’ Golgotha; Judah’s captivity is Jesus’ Calvary.’ (J. Gibson, p.816)「耶路撒冷的傾覆與被擄，就是耶穌的各各他；猶大的擄掠，就是耶穌的十字架。」（J. Gibson，第 816 頁）
Why? Because, like Lady Zion, he too became unclean — defiled by sin. But this time, not his own. He became sin for all who would repent and believe.為甚麼？因為祂也像錫安一樣成了不潔——被罪玷污。但這一次，不是祂自己的罪。祂為一切願意悔改相信的人成為罪。
That is why the ruin, the misery, the devastation of hell is not inevitable. Do not let it happen to you. Let him take it from you.這就是為甚麼地獄的毀滅、痛苦與荒涼，並非無可避免。不要讓它臨到你。讓祂替你承擔。

A groaning city

一座呻吟的城

Lamentations 1:1-22 耶利米哀歌 1:1-22 · Bible Study Guide 聖經學習指南
“Is it nothing to you, all you who pass by?” (Lamentations 1:12) · 「你們一切過路的人哪，這事你們不介意嗎？」（耶利米哀歌 1:12）

Summary 摘要

EN — Lamentations 1 sets us in front of a city that has been utterly reversed: once full of people, now full of groaning. The poet looks on as an observer for the first half, and Lady Zion speaks for herself in the second. Three things press on us. The reversal is brutal — a decade of oppression, months of siege, and then Babylon in the streets. The isolation is crippling — six times we are told there is no one to comfort her; her allies betrayed her, and heaven itself is silent (God does not speak once in the whole book). And the ruin was avoidable — this is not God's indifference but God's justice, after years of pleading that were ignored. The chapter leaves us without comfort on purpose, so that when we meet the one who was also betrayed, also stripped, also alone, also unanswered from heaven — but for our sin and not his own — we understand what he carried.

中文 — 耶利米哀歌第一章把我們帶到一座徹底翻轉的城面前：從前人煙稠密， 如今滿了歎息。前半段是詩人以旁觀者的身分觀看，後半段是錫安自己開口說話。 有三件事壓在我們心上。這翻轉是徹底的——十年的壓迫、數月的圍城， 然後巴比倫的軍隊湧進街上。這孤單是癱瘓人的——「無人安慰」出現了六次； 盟友背叛她，連天也沉默（全卷書神一句話也沒有說）。而這毀滅本是可以避免的 ——這不是神的漠不關心，而是神的公義，在多年的懇勸被置若罔聞之後。 本章刻意不給我們安慰，好叫我們遇見那一位——同樣被朋友出賣、同樣被剝去衣裳、 同樣孤身一人、同樣向天呼喊而無回音的那一位——卻是為我們的罪而非祂自己的罪， 我們才明白祂所擔負的是甚麼。

Key Scriptures 重要經文

  • Lamentations 1:1 — “How deserted lies the city, once so full of people!” 耶利米哀歌 1:1 ——「先前滿有人民的城，現在何竟獨坐！」
  • Lamentations 1:2 — “Among all her lovers there is no one to comfort her. All her friends have betrayed her.” 耶利米哀歌 1:2 ——「在一切所親愛的中間沒有一個安慰她的。 她的朋友都以詭詐待她。」
  • Lamentations 1:12 — “Is it nothing to you, all you who pass by?” 耶利米哀歌 1:12 ——「你們一切過路的人哪，這事你們不介意嗎？」
  • Lamentations 1:18 — “The LORD is righteous, yet I rebelled against his command.” 耶利米哀歌 1:18 ——「耶和華是公義的！ 他這樣待我，是因我違背他的命令。」
  • Romans 12:15 — “Mourn with those who mourn.” 羅馬書 12:15 ——「與哀哭的人要同哭。」
  • Mark 9:48 — “Where their worm does not die and the fire is not quenched.” 馬可福音 9:48 ——「在那裏，蟲是不死的，火是不滅的。」

Key Themes 主要主題

  1. Reversal 翻轉 — nothing is so secure that it cannot change. The poem opens with a city full of people and closes with a city full of groaning. 沒有甚麼穩固到不能改變。詩的開頭是人煙稠密的城， 結尾是滿了歎息的城。
  2. Isolation 孤單 — “no one to comfort her”, six times. Suffering alone is a different order of suffering. 「無人安慰」出現六次。獨自受苦，是另一種層次的受苦。
  3. Consequence 後果 — in a world where God is just, sin must have consequences. Patience is not indifference. 在一個有公義之神的世界裏，罪必有後果。忍耐不等於漠不關心。
  4. Lament as faith 哀歌也是信心 — the book gives us permission and language to voice pain honestly before God. 這卷書給我們許可，也給我們言語，在神面前誠實地說出痛苦。
  5. The greater sufferer 那位更大的受苦者 — verse 12 finds its fullest answer at the cross. 第 12 節在十字架上得著最完滿的回答。

Thinking Questions 思考問題

  1. Read verses 1-7. What details make the reversal feel so total? Which image stays with you? 請讀 1 至 7 節。哪些細節令這翻轉顯得如此徹底？ 哪一個畫面最留在你心中？
  2. “No one to comfort her” appears six times. Why do you think the poet repeats it rather than saying it once? 「無人安慰」出現了六次。 你認為詩人為甚麼不說一次就算，而要一再重複？
  3. God does not speak anywhere in Lamentations. How do you sit with that? Does silence always mean absence? 全卷耶利米哀歌中神沒有說過一句話。 你怎樣面對這一點？沉默是否一定代表不在？
  4. Verse 18 says “The LORD is righteous”. How can Zion say that while still grieving? What does that teach us about honest prayer? 18 節說「耶和華是公義的」。錫安怎能一面哀哭，一面這樣說？ 這教我們甚麼是誠實的禱告？

Prayer 禱告

EN — Father, we have looked at a city with no one to comfort her, and we have heard your silence. Teach us not to mistake your patience for indifference, nor your justice for cruelty. Where we have quietly assumed that consequences would never reach us, wake us now, while there is still time. Make us the people who sit with those who mourn, who hold and listen and stay. And thank you for the Lord Jesus, who was betrayed, stripped, alone and unanswered from heaven — not for his own sin, but for ours. Let him take from us what we could never carry. Amen.
